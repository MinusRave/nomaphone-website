*Updated for 2026: what happened to Skype Credit, a fairer comparison of the alternatives, and current NomaPhone rates.*

If you relied on Skype to call family abroad, reach clients, or get through to your bank's customer service line, you already know: **Skype is gone, and so is Skype calling.**

Microsoft shut Skype down on May 5, 2025. Users were moved to Microsoft Teams Free, which keeps your chats and contacts and handles video calls between users, but it does not call landlines or mobile numbers. The feature many people actually paid for, dialing real phone numbers with Skype Credit, has no direct replacement inside Microsoft's free products.

For digital nomads, expats, and anyone who calls offices, banks or relatives' landlines in another country, that leaves a gap. This guide covers the 7 best Skype alternatives for international calls, with honest pros and cons for each, including where our own product, NomaPhone, is and isn't the right pick.

## Why Skype Shut Down (And Why It Matters)

Microsoft announced the shutdown in early 2025, saying it would focus its consumer communication on Teams. Skype had been losing ground for years to WhatsApp, FaceTime, Zoom and Teams itself, and Microsoft had already stopped selling new Skype Credit and calling subscriptions in the months before the closure.

**What the shutdown changed:**
- Millions of users were moved to Teams Free or left to find another tool
- Teams Free has no pay-as-you-go calling to phone numbers
- Remaining Skype Credit and Skype Numbers could only be used for a limited time, through a web portal, and could no longer be topped up
- Calls to landlines (banks, government offices, businesses) needed a new tool

If you're reading this, you're probably looking for that new tool. Let's find the right one.

## What You Actually Need in a Skype Alternative

Start with what you used Skype for. Most people fall into one of two camps: calling people who have smartphones (free apps solve this), or calling phone numbers that will never be on an app (banks, offices, landlines, toll-free lines). This guide is mostly about the second camp.

Not all international calling services are created equal. Based on our experience (and feedback from hundreds of digital nomads), here's what matters:

**Must-have features:**
- **Calls to landlines and mobile numbers** (not just app-to-app)
- **Works from wherever you are** (some services require US residency to sign up)
- **Coverage of the countries you call** (no service covers every country equally well)
- **Transparent pricing** (landline and mobile rates shown separately, no connection fees)
- **Easy setup** (when your card is blocked, you need to call now)

**Depends on you:**
- A phone number of your own, for receiving calls and SMS codes
- Unlimited plans, if you call one country every day
- No app install, if you call from a work laptop or shared device
- Credit that doesn't expire, if you call only occasionally

Now let's compare the alternatives.

### 2. Google Voice - Best for US Residents

**What it is:** Google's VoIP service with a free US number and free calls to the US and Canada.

### 3. Rebtel - Best for Unlimited Plans

**What it is:** App-based international calling, around since 2006, with pay-as-you-go credit and unlimited plans per country.

**Pricing:**
- Pay-per-minute: varies by country
- Unlimited plans: a monthly fee per destination country

**Pros:**

**Cons:**

- Plans are per country, so it's less suited to calling many different countries
- Fair-use limits on "unlimited" plans

**Best for:** People who make frequent calls to one or two specific countries.

### 5. Yolla and Talk360 - Best Budget Apps for Specific Regions

**What they are:** App-based calling services with pay-as-you-go credit. Yolla is popular for low rates to South Asia and many other countries; Talk360 specializes in calls to Africa.

**Pricing:** Pay-as-you-go; rates vary by destination and are often very low on the routes they focus on.

**Pros:**
- Competitive rates on their core corridors
- Talk360 covers far more African countries than most services, including NomaPhone, which covers only Egypt, Morocco, Nigeria and South Africa
- Simple dialer apps

**Cons:**
- App and account required
- Rates outside their focus regions may not be competitive

**Best for:** Regular calls to one region, especially Africa (Talk360) or South Asia (Yolla).

### 6. WhatsApp and FaceTime - Best Free App-to-App Option

**What they are:** Free voice and video calls between people who have the same app.

**Pricing:** Free over Wi-Fi or mobile data.

**Pros:**
- Completely free
- End-to-end encrypted
- Most of your contacts probably already have one of them

**Cons:**
- **Cannot call landlines or ordinary phone numbers**
- Both people need the app and an internet connection
- Useless for banks, government offices and most businesses

**Best for:** Friends and family with smartphones. If the person you're calling uses WhatsApp or FaceTime, use it; don't pay for a call.

### 7. Your Carrier's International Plan - Best When There's No Internet

**What it is:** International calling add-ons, roaming passes or a local SIM.

**Pricing:** Varies widely; roaming pay-per-minute can run $1 to $3 a minute, and day passes cost around $10 to $12 a day with US carriers at the time of writing.

**Pros:**
- Works without Wi-Fi or data
- Keeps your own number, so people can call you back
- Can call emergency numbers

**Cons:**
- Usually the most expensive way to make the call
- Toll-free numbers often can't be reached from foreign networks

**Best for:** Places with poor internet, emergencies, and calls where your own number must show.

Keep in mind that long calls add up fast on roaming: a 20-minute call to your bank or insurer at $1 to $3 a minute costs $20 to $60, and hold time counts too.

What about old-style calling cards? They still exist, but between access numbers, PINs, connection fees and expiring balances, there's little reason to choose them over any option above.

## What Happens to Your Skype Credit?

When Skype closed, Microsoft let paying users keep using their remaining Skype Credit and active Skype Number subscriptions for a limited time, through the Skype web portal and the dial pad in Teams Free. You can't buy new credit, and access was time-limited.

Policies and deadlines have changed more than once, so check Microsoft's Skype support pages for your account. If you still have a balance, use it while you can. ## Common Questions About Skype Alternatives

**Q: Can I port my Skype Number to another service?**
A: Porting options for Skype Numbers were limited and depended on the country; check Microsoft's support pages. NomaPhone doesn't provide phone numbers, so it can't take a ported number. If you need a number, look at Google Voice (US) or a dedicated number provider.

**Q: What if I need to call emergency services?**
A: Use your local mobile phone. Many internet calling services, including NomaPhone, cannot call 911, 112 or 999.

**Q: Do these services work on hotel or café Wi-Fi?**
A: Usually, yes. Quality depends on how stable the connection is. Use earphones, and switch to mobile data if the Wi-Fi is congested.

**Q: Can I receive calls with these services?**
A: Google Voice can, with a US number. Some paid services sell numbers. NomaPhone can't: it's for outgoing calls only.

**Q: Can a Skype alternative receive my bank's 2FA codes?**
A: Only if it gives you a phone number, and even then many banks reject internet (VoIP) numbers for verification. Calling-only services like NomaPhone don't receive SMS at all. Before you travel, keep your home number alive on a cheap plan or eSIM, move accounts to an authenticator app where possible, and save backup codes. If a code still doesn't arrive, you can call the bank's support line by voice and verify your identity with a person; NomaPhone can place that call from your browser.

**Q: Can I call a US 800 number from abroad?**
A: Often not from a foreign SIM. NomaPhone calls US and Canadian toll-free numbers at its normal US rate. More in our guide to [calling 800 numbers from outside the US](/blog/how-to-call-us-toll-free-numbers-from-anywhere/).
