You're in Thailand (Chiang Mai, Bangkok, Phuket or an island) and you need to call a US number. Maybe your bank, the IRS, your accountant, a client, or a relative who refuses to install WhatsApp.

You can always make the call. The question is how to do it without a painful roaming bill, and in a way that actually reaches the number you need. This guide compares the three main options in 2026: internet calling apps, the phone network (roaming or a Thai SIM), and VoIP/browser calling.

**Quick dialing format:** from a Thai phone, dial an international access code (001, or a carrier code like 003, 004, 005 or 006), then 1, then the 10-digit US number. From a smartphone you can also dial +1 and the number. Example: +1 212 555 0123.

## Method 1: WhatsApp/FaceTime (Free but Limited)

### What It Is
WhatsApp, FaceTime, Signal, LINE (popular in Thailand) and Messenger make free voice and video calls over the internet, but only to other people using the same app.

### When It Works
- Friends and family with smartphones
- Video calls home
- Group calls

### When It Doesn't Work
**You can't call regular phone numbers.** Your US bank, the IRS, an airline, your doctor's office or your accountant's landline can't be reached through WhatsApp or FaceTime. If the person or business only has a phone number, these apps won't help.

### Wi-Fi Calling: The Overlooked Option
If you kept your US phone line active, check whether your carrier supports **Wi-Fi calling** abroad. On many US plans (T-Mobile, AT&T, Verizon and some prepaid carriers), calls to US numbers made over Wi-Fi from abroad are billed like domestic calls. Turn on airplane mode, re-enable Wi-Fi, make sure Wi-Fi calling is on, and dial normally. Check your carrier's current terms, because rules differ by plan and phone, and you still pay to keep the US line active.

### Quality from Thailand
Good on solid Wi-Fi or 4G/5G in cities. Islands and rural areas vary.

### Bottom Line
Free apps are great for people. Wi-Fi calling is great if you already pay for a US line that supports it. Neither helps if you need to call a business and don't have a US plan.

## Method 2: International Roaming (Expensive but Easy)

### Option A: Roaming on your US plan

Use your US number in Thailand and pay your carrier's international rates. Typical structures, at the time of writing (check current pricing):

- **T-Mobile:** many plans include basic data in Thailand; calls are around $0.25/minute.
- **AT&T:** International Day Pass, around $12/day; pay-per-use rates of a few dollars a minute without it.
- **Verizon:** TravelPass, around $12/day; pay-per-use rates of a few dollars a minute without a plan.

**Example:** three 15-minute calls on three different days. On a $12 day pass that's around $36. On T-Mobile's per-minute rate, around $11.

**Advantages:** your phone works normally, your US number shows up, and verification texts from US services arrive on the same phone.
**Disadvantages:** costs climb quickly if you're in Thailand for weeks or months.
**Makes sense for:** very short trips, emergencies, or plans that already include international calling.

### Option B: A Thai SIM with an IDD code

A Thai SIM from AIS, dtac or True gives you a local number, cheap data and the option to call abroad. Each carrier has its own international access code, and the economy codes are usually much cheaper than dialing with "+":

| Carrier | International access codes |
|---|---|
| AIS | 003 (standard), 005 (economy) |
| dtac | 004 |
| True | 006 |
| NT (formerly CAT/TOT) | 001, 007 (among others) |

To call the US: access code + 1 + area code + number, for example 005 1 212 555 0123. Per-minute rates to the US through economy codes are usually a few baht per minute, but they change frequently and depend on your package. Check your carrier's app or dial its help line before relying on it. Note that US toll-free numbers (800, 888 and so on) generally can't be reached from a Thai SIM.

**Advantages:** no internet needed; useful when Wi-Fi is down.
**Disadvantages:** rates and codes vary by carrier and package; no toll-free numbers; your Thai number shows up, which US banks won't recognize.

## Method 3: VoIP/Browser Calling (Cheap and Reliable)

### What It Is
VoIP services let you call regular US phone numbers over the internet, usually for a few cents a minute. Some need an app; browser-based services run in your web browser.

### How It Works
1. Connect to Wi-Fi or mobile data in Thailand.
2. Open the service (app or browser).
3. Dial the US number.
4. The call travels over the internet to the provider, then onto the US phone network.
5. The person answers their regular phone.

### Popular Options

**Google Voice**
Free calls to US numbers, but you need a US number to sign up, it's aimed at US residents, and keeping it working from abroad can be fiddly. See [Google Voice not working abroad](/blog/google-voice-not-working-abroad/).

**App-based services**
Viber Out and similar apps call landlines and mobiles once you install the app and create an account. Skype used to be the default here, but it shut down in May 2025.

**Browser-based services**

Call quality depends mostly on your connection. Bangkok, Chiang Mai, Phuket and Pattaya generally have fast fiber and 4G/5G. Islands with good 4G are usually fine. Overloaded guesthouse Wi-Fi and rural 3G areas are where calls get choppy, for any internet calling service.

### What You Need
- Internet connection (Wi-Fi or mobile data)
- A browser on a computer, tablet or phone
- A microphone (built-in is fine; headphones are better)
- A way to pay

### Disadvantages
**Internet required:** no connection, no call.
**Caller ID:** the call doesn't come from your US number, so the person can't call you back on it, and banks will verify you in other ways.

## Internet Requirements in Thailand

### Minimum Speed Needed
Voice calls need very little bandwidth: around 100 kbps each way works, and a stable connection with low latency matters more than raw speed. Almost any 4G connection is enough.

### Wi-Fi Options
- **Coworking spaces:** usually fast and reliable.
- **Cafés:** fine in cities, variable at peak hours.
- **Hotels and condos:** usually good in cities; test before important calls.
- **Hostels and budget guesthouses:** often overloaded.

### Mobile Data
AIS, True and dtac all have strong 4G/5G coverage in cities, with AIS generally considered the strongest nationwide. dtac and TrueMove H now belong to the same company following their 2023 merger, but still sell under separate brands. Tourist SIMs with generous data cost a few hundred baht, and monthly plans are similar. Mobile data is often more reliable for calls than shared Wi-Fi.

## Thai Phone Numbers: Do You Need One?

**You probably don't need one** if you're on a short stay and only calling home.

**You probably do** if Thai businesses, landlords or delivery drivers need to reach you, you're staying long-term, or you need SMS verification for Thai apps such as banking, Grab or food delivery.

### Getting a Thai SIM Card
Available at airport counters, carrier shops and convenience stores like 7-Eleven. Bring your passport: registration with ID is mandatory, and identity checks have become stricter in recent years. Many travelers buy an eSIM before arriving.

A Thai SIM covers local needs. For US calls, combine it with Wi-Fi calling, WhatsApp, or browser calling.

## Common Problems and Solutions

### "The call quality is choppy"
- Switch from Wi-Fi to mobile data, or the other way round
- Close video streams and uploads
- Use headphones with a microphone
- Move closer to the router or away from crowded networks

### "I can't get through to customer service"
US customer service queues are long everywhere. Call early in the US morning, use the callback option if offered, and note any reference number. If a call is likely to exceed 30 minutes on NomaPhone, ask for a reference number early so you can redial without starting over.

### "They can't hear me well"
Use a headset, find a quieter spot (Thai street noise is real), and check that the browser has microphone permission.

### "The time zone difference is killing me"
Thailand is UTC+7 and has no daylight saving time; the US does.

| | US Eastern business hours (9am-5pm) | US Pacific business hours (9am-5pm) |
|---|---|---|
| March to early November (US daylight time) | 8pm-4am Thailand | 11pm-7am Thailand |
| Early November to March (US standard time) | 9pm-5am Thailand | Midnight-8am Thailand |

Thai evenings are the sweet spot for East Coast calls. For the West Coast, early Thai morning works better.

## Security Considerations in Thailand

### Using Public Wi-Fi
Avoid reading out card numbers, passwords or Social Security numbers in public places, both because of who's listening nearby and because shared networks are less trustworthy. A VPN adds a layer of encryption on public Wi-Fi; test call quality with it on, as some VPNs add latency.

### Privacy in Shared Spaces
In coworking spaces and cafés, step into a phone booth or outside for bank, tax or confidential business calls.

### Scams
Thai and US numbers alike get scam calls. Only call your bank or government agencies on numbers from their official websites or the back of your card.
