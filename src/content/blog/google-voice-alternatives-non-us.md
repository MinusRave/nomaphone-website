## Why Google Voice Doesn't Work Outside the US

Google Voice is a domestic US product. Here's where it falls apart for non-US residents.

**US phone number required for verification.** Creating a Google Voice account requires verification with a US mobile or landline from a major carrier. VoIP numbers, prepaid burners, and international numbers all get rejected. No US number, no account.

**IP address blocking.** Google monitors where you access Voice from. Even existing users report getting locked out after extended time abroad. VPNs work inconsistently — Google's detection keeps improving.

**Inactivity number reclaiming.** Stop using your Google Voice number for several months while abroad and Google may reclaim it. They send a warning email, but miss it and your number is gone.

**Call quality degrades overseas.** Forums and Reddit threads are full of expats in Asia and parts of Europe reporting echo, lag, and dropped calls. Google Voice routing wasn't optimized for intercontinental distances.

**Unreliable international incoming calls.** Google Voice numbers receive calls from US and Canadian numbers, but international callers face spotty reliability.

## What Non-US Residents Actually Need

**Affordable per-minute rates.** Carrier roaming runs $2 to $3 per minute. You need something measured in cents.

**No geographic restrictions.** The service should work from Lisbon, Bangkok, Nairobi, or Berlin without IP blocks.

**SMS for 2FA.** US banks send verification codes via text. If your calling service can receive those, that's a major advantage.

**Browser-based access.** No app to download means any device with a browser and internet works.

**Pay-as-you-go.** Monthly subscriptions waste money if you only call a few times per month.

**Reliability for important calls.** When you're on hold with your bank's fraud department for 40 minutes, the call needs to stay connected.

**Best for:** Heavy callers who don't need SMS/2FA and want recording with transcripts. The penny savings per minute adds up over many calls.

**Limitations:** No SMS/2FA. Chromium-only browser requirement. Fewer supported countries.

### 3. DialAnyone — The Full-Featured Budget Option

Let's be straightforward. DialAnyone is cheaper than NomaPhone. Significantly cheaper. If price is your top priority, look here first.

**How it works.** Browser-based calling plus iOS and Android apps. Sign up, get 10 free credits, start calling. Covers 210+ countries with virtual numbers in 50+.

**Where it works.** 210+ countries. Browser and mobile apps. No geographic restrictions.

**Limitations:** No call recording. The open-source claim is worth investigating before routing sensitive calls through it. Test their free credits to evaluate quality yourself.

### 4. Viber Out — App-Based Calling With Strong Eastern European Rates

Viber Out is the paid calling feature inside the Viber messaging app. If you already use Viber, adding outbound calls is straightforward.

**How it works.** Download the Viber app (iOS, Android, desktop), buy credits, dial any phone number. Calls route through Viber's VoIP network to reach regular landlines and mobiles.

**Pricing.** Varies by destination and changes frequently. US rates are typically higher than browser VoIP services — roughly $0.05/min or more. Both per-minute credits and monthly country plans available.

**SMS/2FA.** No. Outbound calls only.

**Where it works.** Anywhere the Viber app runs. No browser option.

**Best for:** Viber users who call Eastern European countries. Regional plans for Ukraine, Serbia, and Belarus can undercut other options.

**Limitations:** App required. Higher US rates than VoIP alternatives. Credits expire after 180 days of inactivity.

### 5. TextNow — Free US Calling (But Only From US/Canada)

TextNow comes up in every "free calling" search. It offers ad-supported US and Canadian phone numbers with free calls and texts.

**How it works.** Download the app or use their web interface. Get a free US number. Make and receive calls to US/Canadian numbers at no cost. Ad-supported.

**Pricing.** Free for US and Canadian calls. The catch is in the fine print.

**SMS/2FA.** Partial. TextNow numbers receive SMS, but many banks flag them as VoIP and refuse to send verification codes. Results vary by bank.

**Where it works.** Designed for US and Canada. While technically accessible abroad, international users frequently report issues with sign-up, number assignment, and call quality. TextNow may reclaim your number with infrequent use.

**Best for:** People still in the US or Canada who want a free backup number before moving abroad. A stopgap, not a long-term international solution.

**Limitations:** Unreliable outside North America. Banks often reject TextNow numbers for 2FA. Ad-heavy experience. Your data funds the service.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use Google Voice with a VPN from outside the US?

Sometimes. Some people keep Google Voice working abroad with a US-based VPN. But Google detects VPN traffic and may flag your account. It's unreliable, and you still need a US number for initial sign-up.

### Do I need a US phone number to sign up for these alternatives?

No. All five let you create an account without a US phone number. That's their core advantage over Google Voice.

### Which alternative is best for receiving bank 2FA codes?

NomaPhone and DialAnyone both receive SMS verification codes from US banks. TextNow numbers often get flagged as VoIP and rejected by financial institutions. YadaPhone and Viber Out don't support SMS at all.

### Are these services legal outside the US?

Yes. VoIP calling is legal in the vast majority of countries. A few places restrict VoIP services — parts of the Middle East, for example — but most expats and nomads can use these without issues.
