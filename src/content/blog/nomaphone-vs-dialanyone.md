## The Quick Overview

Before we get into the details, here's what each service is in one sentence.

**DialAnyone**, according to its website at the time of writing, is a full-featured international calling platform with apps, virtual numbers in 50+ countries, an eSIM data plan, a REST API, SMS/2FA support, and open-source code. It's aimed at power users and developers who want maximum flexibility at the lowest possible price.

**NomaPhone** is a browser-based "public phone" for outgoing calls: no app to download, no account, prepaid credit. It's built for people who want to open a browser tab and call a landline or mobile, and nothing else. It doesn't give you a number and doesn't receive calls or SMS.

Two different products. Two different philosophies. Same general category.

## Where DialAnyone Wins

Let's be direct about DialAnyone's advantages.

### Price

Several times cheaper on US calls, based on advertised rates. For high-volume callers, that adds up. If you're making hours of calls per month, the savings are meaningful.

### Virtual Numbers in 50+ Countries

Need a local number in Germany, Japan, or Brazil? DialAnyone says it has you covered. NomaPhone doesn't offer phone numbers at all: it's for outgoing calls only. If you need a number, or a local presence in multiple countries, DialAnyone is the obvious choice of the two.

### eSIM Data ($4.99/mo)

This is clever bundling. If you're a nomad who needs both calling and mobile data, getting both from one provider simplifies your setup. NomaPhone doesn't offer data at all.

### Full API with Webhooks

If you're a developer building something — an app that makes calls, an automated system, a customer support tool — DialAnyone's REST API gives you programmable voice. NomaPhone has no API. This isn't even a comparison for developer use cases.

### Open Source

DialAnyone says its codebase is open source, and they claim full encryption and privacy. If code transparency matters to you (and it should), this is a genuine advantage. You can audit what they're doing with your data.

### SMS and 2FA

DialAnyone advertises SMS and 2FA support on its numbers. NomaPhone doesn't receive SMS or 2FA codes at all, so this is a clear DialAnyone advantage. One caveat that applies to any virtual number: some banks don't send codes to VoIP numbers, so check with yours before relying on it.

## Where NomaPhone Wins

NomaPhone's advantages are fewer but specific.

### Zero Friction

Open your browser. Go to NomaPhone. Make a call. That's the entire setup process.

No app to download. No account at all. No API keys to manage. No decisions about which features to enable. You pay with Apple Pay, Google Pay or card, and your credit sits on a phone card code that's also emailed to you.

If you're sitting in a cafe in Lisbon and your bank just sent a fraud alert, you want to be on the phone fast. You don't want to be downloading an app, creating an account, and navigating a feature-rich dashboard.

NomaPhone is built for that moment.

### Browser-Only Simplicity

Some people see "no app" as a limitation. Others see it as the point.

Browser-only means it works on any device with a modern browser. Your laptop, your partner's tablet, a borrowed computer at a coworking space. No installation. No storage space. No keeping apps updated.

DialAnyone works in the browser too, but their full feature set appears to push you toward their mobile apps. NomaPhone is browser-first and browser-only because that's the design choice, not a compromise.

### Credits That Never Expire

You buy $5 in NomaPhone credits. You use $2 this month. The remaining $3 sits there until you need it — whether that's next week or next year. No expiration. No "use it or lose it" pressure.

This matters for people who call irregularly. Maybe you only need international calls when tax season hits, or when your bank locks your card, or when your mom's birthday comes around. You shouldn't have to worry about whether your credits evaporated. And if you decide you don't need the rest, unused credit can be refunded to your original payment method, free.

### US Toll-Free Numbers

Many US banks, airlines and government offices only list 1-800 style numbers, which don't work from a foreign SIM. NomaPhone calls US and Canadian toll-free numbers (800, 888, 877, 866, 855, 844, 833) at its normal US rate. Calls run up to 30 minutes each; if a hold runs longer, you dial again.

No subscriptions. No monthly minimums. No contracts. The rate is shown on screen as you dial, and calls are billed per second.

## Who Should Use DialAnyone

DialAnyone is the better choice if you match this profile:

**You're a developer or power user.** You want API access, webhooks, and the ability to build calling into your own systems. DialAnyone's REST API makes this possible. NomaPhone can't do this at all.

**You need virtual numbers in many countries.** If your work requires local phone numbers in Germany, Japan, Brazil, or dozens of other countries, DialAnyone's virtual number coverage is significantly broader.

**You want eSIM data bundled with calling.** One provider for voice and data simplifies your nomad tech stack. DialAnyone's $4.99/mo eSIM plan is a nice add-on that NomaPhone doesn't offer.

**You make high-volume calls.** If you're on the phone for hours every week, the per-minute savings add up. At $0.005/min, DialAnyone is clearly the budget-friendly choice for heavy callers.

**You care about open source.** Being able to audit the code that handles your calls and data is a legitimate security advantage. If code transparency is a priority, DialAnyone delivers.

**You need to receive calls or SMS.** NomaPhone can't do either. If you need a number for incoming calls or verification codes, DialAnyone (or another number provider) is the one to look at.

**You want maximum features.** Apps, API, virtual numbers, eSIM, SMS — DialAnyone is building a comprehensive communications platform. If you want everything in one place, they offer more.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use both services?

Absolutely. Some people use DialAnyone for a number and high-volume calls, and NomaPhone when they just want to dial from a browser without logging in. There's no contract with either service, so mixing and matching costs you nothing extra.

### Is DialAnyone's call quality worse because it's cheaper?

Not necessarily. Price doesn't always correlate directly with quality in VoIP. Call quality for any VoIP service depends on the route, the time of day and, above all, your own internet connection. We're not going to claim our quality is better, or theirs worse, without data to prove it.

### Does NomaPhone offer API access?

No. It's not available today, and we're not going to sell you on features that don't exist.

### Which service is better for receiving 2FA codes from banks?

DialAnyone, since it advertises SMS-capable numbers. NomaPhone doesn't provide numbers or receive SMS. Where NomaPhone helps is the fallback: when a code doesn't arrive, you can call your bank by voice and sort it out with a person. Either way, check whether your bank accepts VoIP numbers for verification; many don't.

### What if I'm outside the US and need to call a US toll-free number?

NomaPhone handles this: US and Canadian toll-free numbers (800, 888, 877, 866, 855, 844, 833) are charged at the normal US rate of [[rate:US:landline]]. For DialAnyone, check their rate card or support pages.

### Do either service work without internet?

No. Both are VoIP services that require an internet connection. If you're somewhere without reliable WiFi or mobile data, neither service will work. This is a limitation of all browser-based and app-based calling — not specific to either product.
