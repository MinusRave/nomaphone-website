**Pros:**

## Viber Out

**What it is.** The paid calling feature inside the Viber messaging app. Lets you call landlines and mobiles from the Viber app.

**Pricing model.** Credit-based pay-as-you-go. Rates vary widely by destination. Credits expire after 180 days of inactivity.

**Key rates.** Rates vary significantly. US calls are roughly $0.05/min, but pricing changes by destination and fluctuates.

**Pros:**
- Good call quality to Eastern Europe specifically
- If you already use Viber for messaging, it's convenient
- Available on iOS, Android, and desktop

**Cons:**
- App required -- no browser-only option
- Credits expire after 180 days
- Rates are often higher than dedicated VoIP services
- No SMS/2FA receiving
- The app is heavy -- it's a full messaging platform, not just a dialer
- Limited to about 100 countries

**Honest take.** Viber Out makes sense if Viber is already your messaging app and you call Eastern Europe regularly. For everyone else, there are cheaper and more flexible options. The 180-day credit expiration is annoying if you only call internationally occasionally.

## Rebtel

**What it is.** An app-based international calling service that's been around since 2006. Uses a mix of VoIP and local access number routing to improve call quality on spotty connections.

**Pricing model.** Both pay-as-you-go and unlimited calling plans to specific countries. Plans start around $5-10/month depending on destination. Pay-as-you-go rates are roughly $0.04-0.08/min.

**Pros:**
- Unlimited plans genuinely useful if you call one country frequently
- Local number routing improves quality on poor internet
- 20 years in business -- proven reliability
- Hybrid VoIP/phone network routing

**Cons:**
- App required -- no browser option
- Confusing pricing (different plans per country, fair-use limits on "unlimited")
- Only 50+ countries covered
- No SMS/2FA receiving
- Per-country model doesn't suit nomads calling multiple countries

**Honest take.** Rebtel is built for one use case: calling one country a lot. If you're an NRI calling India daily, or a UK expat calling home every evening, the unlimited plans save real money. But the per-country model is a poor fit for nomads calling the US, UK, and India in the same week.

**Key rates.** Rates vary. Best value is on calls to African nations. US and European rates are not a strong point.

**Key rates.** Plan-dependent. Consumer rates vary by destination.

**Pros:**

- SMS support, call recording, virtual numbers
- 200+ countries

**Cons:**

## Which Service Fits Your Situation

Not everyone needs the same thing. Here's a quick guide based on common use cases.

### Best for Expats Who Need Bank Access

**NomaPhone** or **DialAnyone.**

If you're living abroad and need to call US banks, receive SMS verification codes, and stay connected to financial institutions, you need SMS/2FA support. Only three services on this list offer it: NomaPhone, DialAnyone, and Google Voice (US only). Between NomaPhone and DialAnyone, it comes down to whether you prioritize reliability and simplicity (NomaPhone) or lowest price and maximum features (DialAnyone).

### Best for Digital Nomads

**NomaPhone** or **YadaPhone.**

Nomads need something that works in any country, on any device, without installing anything. Browser-based services win here. NomaPhone covers 210+ countries with SMS/2FA. YadaPhone is cheaper per minute but limited to Chromium browsers and lacks SMS support. If you switch between devices and browsers often, NomaPhone's universal browser support matters.

### Cheapest Overall

**DialAnyone.**

At $0.005/min to the US, DialAnyone is the clear price leader. A 30-minute call costs 15 cents. A heavy calling month costs about a dollar. If budget is your primary concern and you're comfortable with a newer service, DialAnyone is hard to argue with.

### Best Free Option

**Google Voice.**

Free calls to US and Canada, no per-minute charges. The catch: you need to be a US resident with a US phone number to set it up. If you already have it and you're calling US numbers, use it. Quality from Asia is hit-or-miss, so have a backup ready.

### Best for Business

**Vonage** or **YadaPhone.**

If you need call recording, team features, shared billing, or a proper business phone setup, these two serve that need. Vonage is the heavyweight with full business plans. YadaPhone is lighter with AI transcription and team wallets. For solo freelancers, YadaPhone is probably enough. For a company with multiple employees, Vonage is more appropriate.

### Best for Calls to One Country

**Rebtel.**

If you call the same country every day -- parents in India, partner in the UK, family in Mexico -- Rebtel's unlimited plans can save money over pay-as-you-go pricing. The per-country model only makes sense for this specific pattern.

### Best for Calls to Africa

**Talk360.**

No contest. Talk360 has the best rates and reliability for calls to South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, and other African nations. For this specific corridor, it's the right tool.

## What About Call Quality?

Price is easy to compare. Quality is harder.

**Browser-based services** (NomaPhone, YadaPhone, DialAnyone) deliver consistent audio over decent WiFi. Coffee shop with reasonable internet is fine. Airport WiFi with 500 people is not.

**App-based services** (Viber Out, Rebtel, Talk360) have similar internet dependence, but sometimes compress audio more aggressively. **Google Voice** has persistent echo and lag from Asia. **Rebtel** has a useful trick: local number routing that bypasses some VoIP quality issues by running part of the call through traditional phone networks.

The honest reality: over reasonable WiFi, most of these sound fine. The differences show up at the margins -- long hold times, poor connections, congested networks. That's when carrier-grade routing versus cheapest-possible routing makes a noticeable difference.
