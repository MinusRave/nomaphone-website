## Lisbon's Internet Infrastructure: The Good News

Here's something that makes Lisbon different from many nomad destinations. The internet is genuinely good.

Portugal invested heavily in fiber optics over the past decade. Lisbon has widespread FTTH (fiber to the home) coverage, and most cafes, coworking spaces, and apartments offer speeds between 100-500 Mbps. Some newer buildings have gigabit connections.

What this means for calling: VoIP and browser-based calling work reliably across the city. You won't deal with the choppy audio or dropped calls that plague nomads in places with weaker infrastructure. Even cafe WiFi in touristy areas like Chiado or Baixa is usually stable enough for a business call.

Mobile data is solid too. 4G covers essentially all of Lisbon, and 5G is expanding through the city center. If your coworking WiFi hiccups mid-call, your phone's hotspot is a reliable backup.

## Local SIM Cards: MEO, NOS, and Vodafone PT

Getting a Portuguese SIM is straightforward. You can buy prepaid SIMs at carrier shops, electronics stores, or even some convenience shops. You'll need your passport.

### MEO

Portugal's largest carrier with the widest coverage. MEO prepaid plans start around 10-15 EUR/month for data packages with decent allowances. International calling rates from MEO are steep though -- expect 0.80-1.20 EUR/minute to the US and 0.60-0.90 EUR/minute to the UK. Brazil is slightly cheaper at 0.40-0.70 EUR/minute.

### NOS

Strong coverage in Lisbon and Porto. NOS prepaid data plans are competitive, and they sometimes offer slightly better international bundles. Calling rates are similar to MEO for most destinations. NOS has good 5G coverage in central Lisbon.

### Vodafone PT

Reliable network with good data speeds. Vodafone Portugal's prepaid options include some international calling bundles, but the per-minute rates without a bundle match MEO and NOS -- around 0.80-1.00 EUR/minute to the US.

### The Verdict on Local SIMs

A Portuguese SIM is great for data. Use it for internet access, hotspotting, and navigation. But don't use it for international calls. The per-minute rates will drain your credit fast. A 30-minute call to the US on any Portuguese carrier costs 24-36 EUR. That's roughly $26-39 USD.

Use your local SIM for data. Use something else for international calls.

## EU Roaming Rules: A Major Advantage

If you already have a SIM from another EU country, here's something worth knowing. Under EU "Roam Like at Home" regulations, you can use your EU SIM in Portugal at domestic rates. That means calls, texts, and data at whatever you pay at home.

This matters if you have, say, a German or French SIM with a good international calling add-on. Your German plan's rates apply while you're in Lisbon, not Portuguese roaming rates.

There are limits. Fair use policies mean carriers can restrict prolonged roaming (usually after 4 months of continuous use outside your home country). And "Roam Like at Home" only covers usage within the EU/EEA -- it doesn't make calls to the US or Brazil cheaper unless your home plan already includes those destinations.

For US or UK nomads, EU roaming rules don't help you directly. Your American or British carrier will still charge international roaming rates in Portugal. But if you pick up a cheap EU SIM from a country with good international plans, you could use it across Portugal and the rest of Europe.

## Coworking Spaces With Good Call Rooms

Lisbon's coworking scene is mature. Several spaces specifically cater to nomads who need to make calls. Here are spots known for reliable calling setups.

### Second Home Lisboa

Located in Mercado da Ribeira (Time Out Market building) in Cais do Sodre. Second Home has a distinctive design and solid WiFi. They offer phone booths and private rooms that work well for calls. The WiFi is reliable and fast enough for VoIP. The location is central, and the atmosphere is professional without being stuffy.

### Outsite Lisbon

In the Cais do Sodre neighborhood. Outsite combines coliving and coworking, and their workspace includes dedicated call rooms. The internet connection is consistently good. If you're staying at their coliving space, you have 24/7 access to the calling rooms -- handy for late-night calls to US West Coast contacts.

### Heden

A popular coworking space with multiple Lisbon locations. Heden offers soundproofed phone booths specifically designed for calls. Their WiFi is business-grade, and staff maintain it actively. The booths are bookable, so you won't be fighting for space during peak hours.

### General Tips

Most Lisbon coworking spaces have WiFi speeds of 100+ Mbps. Even smaller spaces like Selina or Resves Studio offer decent connections. The key differentiator is soundproofing. If you make frequent international calls, prioritize spaces with dedicated phone booths or bookable call rooms. An open-plan desk with great WiFi won't help if background noise drowns out your bank's automated system.

## WiFi Calling From Lisbon

If your US or UK carrier supports WiFi calling, you can use it in Lisbon to make calls over WiFi instead of the cellular network. The call routes through your home carrier's network, so it's treated as a domestic call from your home country.

### The Catch

WiFi calling abroad is inconsistent. Some carriers enable it internationally, others block it. And even when it works, the call quality depends on your WiFi connection and your carrier's VoIP routing.

- **T-Mobile (US):** WiFi calling generally works abroad. Calls register as domestic US calls.
- **AT&T (US):** WiFi calling may work abroad, but AT&T has been known to restrict it in some countries. Portugal usually works.
- **Verizon (US):** WiFi calling abroad is hit-or-miss. Some users report success in Portugal, others don't.
- **EE/Vodafone/Three (UK):** WiFi calling policies vary. Check with your specific carrier before relying on it.

WiFi calling is worth trying, but don't make it your only plan. If it works, great -- free calls home. If it doesn't, you need a backup.

## VoIP in Portugal: No Restrictions

Some countries restrict or throttle VoIP services. Portugal doesn't. This is a big deal.

You can use any VoIP or browser-based calling service in Lisbon without worrying about blocks, throttling, or legal issues. WhatsApp calls, Zoom, Google Meet, browser-based calling services -- all work without restrictions.

Combined with Lisbon's strong internet infrastructure, this makes VoIP the most practical option for international calling from the city. No workarounds needed, no VPN required, no gray areas.

The US is the most common destination for nomads in Lisbon. Bank calls, tax prep, client meetings, family check-ins.

**Time zone consideration:** Portugal uses WET (Western European Time), which is UTC+0 in winter and WEST (UTC+1) in summer. That puts Lisbon 5 hours ahead of US Eastern and 8 hours ahead of US Pacific. If you need to call a US bank that closes at 5 PM Eastern, your window closes at 10 PM Lisbon time. Workable, but plan around it.

### Calling Brazil From Lisbon

Lisbon has a large Brazilian community, and many nomads have business or personal connections in Brazil.

**Time zone:** Brazil spans multiple time zones. Brasilia time (BRT) is UTC-3, putting it 3 hours behind Lisbon in winter and 4 hours behind in summer. Manageable for business calls.

**Rates:** VoIP rates to Brazil vary more than US/UK rates. Browser-based services typically charge $0.05-0.15/minute to Brazilian landlines and $0.10-0.25/minute to mobiles. Portuguese carrier rates to Brazil are 0.40-0.70 EUR/minute. VoIP saves you roughly 80-90% on Brazil calls.

## Time Zone Strategy for Lisbon Nomads

Portugal's time zone position is one of its underrated advantages for nomads.

**US East Coast (New York, Miami, DC):** 5 hours ahead. A 9 AM meeting in New York is 2 PM in Lisbon. A 5 PM call in New York is 10 PM in Lisbon. You can cover most of the US business day without waking up at absurd hours.

**US West Coast (LA, SF, Seattle):** 8 hours ahead. A 9 AM meeting in LA is 5 PM in Lisbon. This is tighter but still workable. You can handle afternoon US West Coast calls in your Lisbon evening.

**UK (London):** Same time zone. No adjustment needed. This is rare and valuable.

**Brazil (Sao Paulo, Rio):** 3-4 hours ahead. A 9 AM meeting in Sao Paulo is noon or 1 PM in Lisbon. Very comfortable overlap.

**India:** 5.5 hours behind Lisbon. Morning calls in Lisbon catch the afternoon in India.

This makes Lisbon one of the best time zone positions for nomads who work with clients across the Americas, Europe, and parts of Asia.

## Setting Up Your Lisbon Calling Stack

Here's a practical setup guide for your first week in Lisbon.

### Day One: Get a Portuguese SIM

Head to a MEO, NOS, or Vodafone store. There's one in practically every shopping center and on most major streets in Baixa, Chiado, and Saldanha. Bring your passport. Get a prepaid data plan -- 15-20 EUR/month gets you plenty of data. Don't add an international calling package. You won't need it.

### Day One: Test Your WiFi Calling

Once you're connected, try enabling WiFi calling on your home carrier phone. If it works, use it for quick calls home. If it doesn't, no loss.

### Day One: Set Up Browser-Based Calling

Open a browser-based calling service. Add credit. Test a short call to a number you know. Confirm the audio quality works on your laptop and on your phone's browser. This takes about two minutes.

### Week One: Find Your Calling Spot

Test calls from your apartment, your coworking space, and a couple of cafes. Figure out where you get the best audio quality and the least background noise. For important bank calls, a coworking phone booth beats a Bairro Alto cafe every time.

## Common Questions From Lisbon Nomads

### Can I Keep My US Number Active While in Lisbon?

Yes. Keep your US SIM active (or port your number to Google Voice before you leave). Having a US number matters for 2FA codes and bank verification. Some browser-based calling services also offer US virtual numbers for receiving calls.

### Is WhatsApp Enough for International Calls?

For personal calls where both sides use WhatsApp, yes. But WhatsApp can't call landlines, toll-free numbers, or businesses that don't use it. You can't call Chase Bank on WhatsApp. You need something that dials actual phone numbers.

### Do I Need a VPN for VoIP in Lisbon?

No. Portugal has no VoIP restrictions. Every calling service works normally. Save your VPN for countries that actually block VoIP.

### What About Calling Portuguese Numbers?

Your Portuguese SIM handles this. Local calls within Portugal are cheap or included in your plan. Use your SIM for local calls and VoIP for international ones.

### How Reliable Is Cafe WiFi for Calls?

In central Lisbon, surprisingly reliable. Most tourist-area cafes have decent WiFi. But "decent for browsing" and "decent for a 45-minute bank call" are different standards. For important calls, use your coworking space or your apartment's WiFi. For casual calls, most cafes work fine.
