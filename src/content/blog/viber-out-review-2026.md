## What Is Viber Out?

Viber Out is the paid calling feature inside the Viber messaging app. It lets you call landlines and mobile phones — real phone numbers, not just other Viber users — from within the app.

The basics:

- You buy calling credits through the Viber app
- You dial a phone number like you would on a regular phone
- The call routes over the internet (VoIP), not your carrier
- Rates vary by destination country

Think of it as a calling layer bolted onto a messaging app. The free Viber-to-Viber calls are the main product. Viber Out is the side feature for reaching people who aren't on Viber.

That distinction matters. Viber Out isn't a standalone calling service — it's an add-on. And that shows in how it's built, priced, and maintained.

## How Viber Out Pricing Works

Viber Out uses a credit-based system. You buy credits in advance, then spend them as you make calls. Rates vary by country, and there are two pricing tiers:

- **Per-minute rates** — Pay for exactly what you use
- **Calling plans** — Monthly subscriptions for specific countries (unlimited or high-minute bundles)

Here's where it gets complicated. Viber Out rates aren't consistent. The per-minute cost to the same country can change depending on when you check, and the difference between calling a landline versus a mobile in the same country can be significant.

Some sample Viber Out rates we found (these fluctuate, so check the app for current pricing):

|---|---|---|
| USA/Canada | $0.019/min | Competitive for US calls |
| UK Landline | $0.019/min | Reasonable |
| UK Mobile | $0.069/min | Jumps significantly for mobile |

| Germany Landline | $0.019/min | Affordable |
| Germany Mobile | $0.099/min | Higher for mobile |

The landline rates look attractive. But mobile rates in many countries jump to two, three, or even five times higher. Since most people you'd call internationally are on mobile phones, those higher rates are what you'll actually pay most of the time.

### Connection Fees and Hidden Costs

Viber Out charges a connection fee on some calls. It's not always obvious upfront. When you're comparing rates, make sure you account for this — a $0.019/min rate with a $0.05 connection fee changes the math on short calls significantly.

Credits do expire if your account is inactive for an extended period. The exact policy has changed over the years, so check the current terms before loading up a large balance.

## The Pros: Where Viber Out Shines

Let's be fair. Viber Out has real strengths.

### Strong Eastern European Coverage

If you're calling countries like Ukraine, Moldova, Georgia, or Armenia, Viber Out is genuinely good. Viber has deep market penetration in Eastern Europe and the former Soviet Union. Call quality to these regions tends to be solid, and rates are competitive.

This makes sense historically. Viber was founded by Israeli-Belarusian entrepreneurs and gained early traction in Eastern Europe before expanding globally. That regional strength remains.

### Large User Base

Viber claims over a billion registered users. In practical terms, this means many of the people you call might already have Viber installed, allowing free Viber-to-Viber calls. Viber Out becomes the fallback for reaching people who aren't on the app, which is a natural extension.

### Familiar App Experience

If you already use Viber for messaging, adding Viber Out credits is seamless. There's no second app to install, no separate account to create. You just buy credits and start dialing from the same interface you use for chats.

### Caller ID

Viber Out lets you set a caller ID number so the person you're calling sees a real number instead of "Unknown." This is helpful when calling businesses or anyone who screens unfamiliar calls.

## The Cons: Where Viber Out Falls Short

Here's where the honest part of this Viber Out review comes in.

### You Must Install the Viber App

This is the biggest friction point. Viber Out only works inside the Viber app. There's no browser-based option, no lightweight web interface. You need to download the full Viber application on your phone or desktop.

For a quick call — like phoning your bank about a fraud alert — downloading and setting up a messaging app you might not otherwise use is a real barrier. Especially if you're in a hurry or on a device that isn't yours.

Browser-based calling services let you make a call in under a minute from any device with a web browser. No download, no account verification tied to a phone number. That's a meaningful difference when you just need to make one call right now.

### Inconsistent Rate Transparency

Finding the exact rate for a specific call before you make it takes some digging. The Viber Out rate page exists, but rates change, and the in-app display doesn't always match the website. You sometimes don't know exactly what a call will cost until you've made it.

Compare this to services that show you the per-minute rate clearly before you dial, with no connection fees or variable pricing tiers.

### Call Quality Is Hit or Miss

Our experience with Viber Out call quality was mixed. Calls to the US and Western Europe were generally fine — clear audio, minimal delay. But calls to Southeast Asia, parts of Africa, and some Latin American mobile numbers had noticeable issues:

- Audio delay (1-2 seconds, making conversation awkward)
- Occasional one-way audio (you can hear them, they can't hear you)
- Calls dropping after 10-15 minutes

This isn't unique to Viber Out. All VoIP services have variable quality depending on routing. But we noticed it more with Viber Out than with some dedicated calling services, possibly because Viber Out isn't the primary product Viber is optimizing.

### Phone Number Required for Account

You need a working phone number to create a Viber account. If you're a nomad who's just swapped SIMs, or if you're on a data-only eSIM without a phone number, this creates a chicken-and-egg problem. You need a phone number to get Viber, but you might want Viber Out because you don't have reliable phone service.

### No SMS or 2FA Support

Viber Out is for voice calls only. You can't receive SMS messages, which means you can't use it for two-factor authentication codes from banks or government services. If you need to receive a text from your bank to verify a transaction while abroad, Viber Out won't help.

### Desktop Experience Is Clunky

The Viber desktop app exists, but it requires pairing with your phone. If your phone is dead, lost, or on a different SIM, the desktop app becomes difficult to use independently. It's designed as a companion to the mobile app, not a standalone calling solution.

### The Real Question: What Are You Optimizing For?

If you're a regular Viber user who mainly calls Eastern Europe and occasionally the US, Viber Out makes sense. It's already on your phone, the rates are decent for those corridors, and there's zero additional setup.

But if you're a digital nomad, expat, or remote worker who needs to call banks, government offices, or businesses in various countries, the app requirement and inconsistent pricing become real friction points.

The scenario that exposes Viber Out's weakness: You're at an airport, your card just got frozen, and you need to call your bank. With Viber Out, you need the Viber app installed and logged in on the device you're using. With a browser-based service, you open any browser, go to a website, and dial.

## Who Should Use Viber Out?

**Viber Out is a good fit if you:**

- Already use Viber daily for messaging
- Primarily call Eastern Europe, the US, or Western Europe
- Want one app for messaging and occasional calling
- Don't need SMS/2FA receiving
- Have Viber installed on all your devices

**Viber Out is NOT a good fit if you:**

- Need to make calls from shared or borrowed devices
- Want transparent, predictable pricing across all destinations
- Need SMS/2FA support for bank verifications
- Prefer not to install a full messaging app just to make calls
- Need reliable desktop calling without phone pairing
- Call a wide variety of countries (rates vary wildly)

## Viber Out Tips If You Decide to Use It

A few things we learned that might save you frustration:

**Check rates in the app before calling.** The website rates and app rates occasionally differ. Always check the in-app rate display for the specific number you're about to call.

**Buy smaller credit amounts.** Don't load $50 in credits at once. Buy $5-10 at a time until you're confident you'll use the service regularly. This protects you from credit expiration policies.

**Test call quality before important calls.** Make a short test call to the country you need before sitting on hold with your bank for 45 minutes. If the quality is poor to that destination, you'll want a backup plan.

**Keep a browser-based backup.** Even if Viber Out is your primary calling method, bookmark a browser-based calling service for emergencies. When you absolutely need to make a call and Viber is giving you trouble, having a backup that works in any browser is worth the peace of mind.

**Use Wi-Fi whenever possible.** Like all VoIP services, Viber Out works best on a stable connection. Airport Wi-Fi and hotel connections can be unpredictable. If you're making an important call, try to find a reliable connection first.

## The Bottom Line

Viber Out is a decent calling feature attached to a popular messaging app. It's not a scam, it's not overpriced for every destination, and it works reasonably well for the regions where Viber is strong.

But it's also not a dedicated calling service. It's a feature, and it shows. The app requirement, inconsistent rates across destinations, variable call quality, and lack of SMS support make it a compromise rather than a solution.

If you already live in Viber, adding Viber Out credits is a no-brainer for occasional calls. If you're downloading Viber specifically to use Viber Out, you might want to look at services that are built from the ground up for international calling — especially browser-based options that skip the app requirement entirely.

The best Skype replacement depends on what you actually need. For casual calls to Eastern Europe, Viber Out works. For reliable calling to banks, government offices, and businesses across dozens of countries, a dedicated service will serve you better.
