## Why Medellin Works So Well for International Calls

Before diving into methods, it helps to understand why Medellin is genuinely one of the easier places to handle international calling as a remote worker.

### Internet Infrastructure

Medellin has some of the best internet in Latin America. Fiber connections are widely available throughout El Poblado, Laureles, and Envigado. Many apartments come with 100-300 Mbps fiber included in rent. That matters because VoIP and browser-based calling need stable internet to work reliably.

Providers like Claro, Tigo-UNE, and ETB offer residential fiber packages. If you're renting through Airbnb or a local agency, ask about the internet provider and speed before booking. Most nomad-friendly neighborhoods already have fiber installed.

4G coverage blankets the metro area, and 5G is rolling out in select zones. You won't struggle to find a stable connection for calls.

### The Time Zone Advantage

This is Medellin's secret weapon for anyone working with US clients or businesses.

Colombia runs on COT (Colombia Time), which is GMT-5. That's the same as US Eastern Time year-round. Colombia doesn't observe daylight saving time, so during US daylight saving months (March through November), Medellin is one hour behind Eastern — still close enough for easy scheduling.

What this means in practice: when your bank in New York opens at 9 AM, it's 9 AM (or 8 AM during DST) in Medellin. No 3 AM alarm clocks. No calculating 12-hour time differences. You can call US businesses during normal hours while keeping a normal schedule yourself.

For UK calls, Medellin is 5-6 hours behind depending on the season. Still workable for afternoon calls.

### Coworking and WiFi Spots

Medellin's coworking scene is mature. Options include:

**Selina** in El Poblado offers day passes and has reliable WiFi designed for calls. The dedicated phone booths and quiet rooms help when you need privacy for a bank call or client meeting.

**WeWork** has a Medellin location with the standard WeWork setup — fast internet, phone booths, professional environment. Good if you already have a WeWork membership from another city.

**Local spaces** are often better value. Spaces like Tinkko, Workia (Laureles and Poblado locations), and La Casa Redonda cater specifically to remote workers and digital nomads. Many include soundproofed call rooms. Day passes typically run 25,000-50,000 COP ($6-12 USD).

**Cafes** work in a pinch, but background noise can be an issue for important calls. Pergamino in El Poblado and Urbania in Laureles have decent WiFi, but don't count on them for a 45-minute hold with your bank's fraud department.

## Local SIM Cards: Claro, Movistar, and Tigo

Getting a Colombian SIM is straightforward. You'll need your passport, and you can pick one up at carrier stores, shopping malls, or even some convenience shops.

### Claro

The largest carrier in Colombia. Best overall coverage, including rural areas outside Medellin. Prepaid plans (prepago) are easy to activate. Data packages are affordable — around 20,000-35,000 COP ($5-9 USD) for several gigabytes.

International call rates from Claro prepaid: roughly 4,000-5,000 COP per minute to the US ($1.00-1.25 USD). Expensive for anything beyond a quick call.

### Movistar

Second largest carrier. Similar coverage to Claro in urban Medellin. Prepaid plans and data packages are comparable. International rates are in the same ballpark — around 4,500 COP per minute to the US.

### Tigo

Third major carrier. Strong in Medellin specifically since Tigo merged with UNE (a Medellin-based provider). Good local coverage and competitive data plans. International calling rates are similar to the other two.

### The SIM Verdict

A local SIM is essential for data, local calls, ride-hailing apps, and receiving Colombian messages. But using it for international calls is expensive. Think of your Colombian SIM as a data pipe for VoIP and browser calling, not as your international calling solution.

## VoIP and Browser-Based Calling From Medellin

Here's the good news: VoIP works well in Colombia.

Some countries block or throttle VoIP services. Colombia isn't one of them. There are no government restrictions on internet calling, and ISPs don't typically interfere with VoIP traffic. If your internet connection is stable, your calls will be stable.

This makes Medellin an excellent city for browser-based and app-based international calling.

Services like Viber Out require downloading an app first. Rates vary by destination. The apps work fine in Colombia — no blocking or throttling — but you need to install and set up the app before you can call.

### Google Voice

If you're a US resident with a Google Voice number, you can use it from Medellin through the web interface or app. Calls to US and Canadian numbers are free. Quality is generally acceptable from Colombia, though some users report occasional lag.

The catch: you need to be a US resident to sign up, and Google periodically reclaims numbers that don't make US-based calls. If you're abroad long-term, your number may be at risk.

## How to Set Up International Calling in Medellin

Getting yourself ready to make international calls from Medellin takes about 15 minutes of setup on your first day.

### Step 1: Get Your Colombian SIM

Head to a Claro, Movistar, or Tigo store with your passport. Centro Comercial El Tesoro, Santafe Mall, or any standalone carrier shop will work. Buy a prepaid SIM and load a data package. Budget 30,000-40,000 COP ($7-10) for the SIM plus a reasonable data allotment.

This gives you local data for everything — maps, ride apps, messaging, and most importantly, a data connection for VoIP calling when WiFi isn't available.

### Step 2: Test Your Internet

Before you need to make an urgent call, test your calling setup on a non-critical call. Try calling a friend, a business with a toll-free number, or any number where you won't mind if quality is rough.

Test from your apartment WiFi, your coworking space, and your mobile data. Know which connection gives you the best call quality before your bank's fraud department needs to verify your identity.

### Step 3: Choose Your Calling Method

For occasional calls (a few per month), browser-based calling is the simplest option. No app to manage, no subscription. Open the browser, dial, talk.

For frequent calls, you might want both a browser service and Google Voice (if eligible). Use Google Voice for quick US calls and a paid service for international numbers, UK calls, or when you need guaranteed reliability.

### Step 4: Save Key Numbers

Store the direct-dial numbers for your bank, credit card company, insurance, and any other critical US or UK contacts. Many toll-free numbers (800, 888, etc.) don't work from international services. Look up the direct international numbers instead. Your bank's website usually lists these on the "Contact Us" page under "Calling from outside the US."

## Practical Tips for International Calling From Medellin

These tips come from the realities of living and working in the city.

### Power and Internet Reliability

Medellin's power grid is generally stable, but outages happen — usually brief. EPM (Empresas Publicas de Medellin) manages utilities and is considered one of Colombia's best utility companies. Still, keep your phone charged as a backup hotspot.

If your apartment loses internet, your Colombian SIM's data plan becomes your backup. This is another reason to keep an active data package loaded.

### Noise Considerations

Medellin is not a quiet city. Motorcycles, construction, reggaeton from neighboring apartments, and street vendors are part of daily life. For important calls:

- Use a headset with a microphone. AirPods or any decent earbuds help isolate your voice.
- Find an interior room away from street-facing windows.
- Coworking phone booths exist for a reason. Use them for bank calls and client meetings.
- Early morning (before 8 AM) tends to be quieter in residential neighborhoods.

### Neighborhood Differences

**El Poblado** has the most coworking options and the densest concentration of nomad-friendly cafes. Internet infrastructure is excellent. It's also the noisiest neighborhood at night due to nightlife.

**Laureles** is quieter, more residential, and increasingly popular with remote workers. Coworking options are growing. Internet quality is comparable to Poblado. Workia Laureles is a solid option for a dedicated workspace with call rooms.

**Envigado** borders El Poblado to the south. Quieter still, with good internet infrastructure. Fewer coworking spaces but more affordable apartments, often with fiber included.

### Calling US Banks and Financial Institutions

US banks are the most common international calling need for Americans in Medellin. A few specific tips:

- Call early. 9 AM Eastern (which is 9 AM or 8 AM in Medellin depending on DST) means shorter hold times.
- Have your account info ready. You'll verify identity faster.
- Use a stable WiFi connection, not mobile data. Bank fraud calls can run 30-60 minutes with holds.
- If your bank requires a callback to a US number, a virtual US number through a VoIP service handles this. Some browser calling services let you receive callbacks on a US number.

### Dealing With 2FA and Verification Codes

Two-factor authentication can be tricky abroad. If your US bank sends SMS verification codes to your US number, you need a way to receive those texts.

Options: keep your US SIM active on a cheap plan, use a service that forwards SMS, or switch to app-based 2FA (Google Authenticator, Authy) before you leave the US. Some VoIP services — including NomaPhone — support receiving SMS and 2FA codes on a US number.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does VoIP work reliably in Medellin?

Yes. Colombia doesn't block or throttle VoIP. With a decent internet connection — fiber in your apartment or coworking WiFi — call quality is consistently good. Mobile data (4G/5G) also works for calls, though WiFi is more reliable for long conversations.

### Can I keep my US phone number while living in Medellin?

You have options. Google Voice (for US residents) lets you maintain a US number. Some VoIP services offer virtual US numbers. You can also keep your US carrier active on a minimal plan and forward calls. The key is having a way to receive US calls and texts — especially for 2FA codes from banks.

### Is Medellin safe for digital nomads in 2026?

This is a calling guide, not a safety guide, but briefly: Medellin has improved dramatically over the past two decades. El Poblado, Laureles, and Envigado are generally safe for foreign workers exercising normal urban precautions. Use common sense, stay aware of your surroundings, and don't flash expensive electronics in unfamiliar areas.

### What about calling other Colombian cities from Medellin?

Local and national calls within Colombia are cheap on any carrier SIM. Your Claro, Movistar, or Tigo plan handles this without issue. VoIP is only necessary for international calls.

### Do I need a VPN in Colombia?

Not for calling purposes. Colombia doesn't restrict internet access or VoIP services. A VPN might be useful for accessing US-only streaming content, but it's not needed for international calls. In fact, a VPN can sometimes add latency and reduce call quality, so turn it off when making calls.
