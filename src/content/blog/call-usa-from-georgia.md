## Georgia's Telecom Landscape

Georgia has three major mobile carriers. All of them offer prepaid SIMs that are easy to buy with a passport.

### Magti

Georgia's largest carrier with the widest coverage.

**International calling rates to USA:**
- Standard: 1.20 GEL/min (~$0.45)
- With international package: 0.40-0.60 GEL/min (~$0.15-0.23)
- SMS to USA: 0.30 GEL (~$0.11)

**Prepaid data plans:**
- 10 GB: 15 GEL/month (~$5.60)
- 25 GB: 25 GEL/month (~$9.40)
- Unlimited: 35 GEL/month (~$13.10)

**Digital nomad verdict:** Best coverage outside Tbilisi. International rates are steep without add-on packages.

### Geocell (Silknet Mobile)

Second-largest, strong in urban areas.

**International calling rates to USA:**
- Standard: 1.00 GEL/min (~$0.37)
- With international package: 0.35-0.55 GEL/min (~$0.13-0.21)

**Prepaid data plans:**
- 10 GB: 12 GEL/month (~$4.50)
- 30 GB: 22 GEL/month (~$8.25)
- Unlimited: 30 GEL/month (~$11.25)

**Digital nomad verdict:** Slightly cheaper than Magti. Good coverage in Tbilisi, Batumi, and major towns.

### Beeline Georgia

Budget-friendly option, smallest network.

**International calling rates to USA:**
- Standard: 0.90 GEL/min (~$0.34)
- Limited international packages available

**Prepaid data plans:**
- 8 GB: 10 GEL/month (~$3.75)
- 20 GB: 18 GEL/month (~$6.75)

**Digital nomad verdict:** Cheapest for data. Coverage gaps outside cities. Fine if you stay in Tbilisi.

### Getting a Georgian SIM

**Requirements:**

- Activation: usually instant

**Process:**
1. Walk into a Magti, Geocell, or Beeline shop
2. Show passport

Georgia makes this easy. No registration hassles, no IMEI headaches, no waiting periods.

## Time Zone Considerations

Georgia uses GET (Georgia Standard Time, UTC+4) year-round. No daylight saving changes.

### Calling US East Coast

- Tbilisi 16:00 = New York 08:00 (EST/EDT)
- Tbilisi 22:00 = New York 14:00 (EST/EDT)
- Best window: 16:00-01:00 Tbilisi time

### Calling US West Coast

- Tbilisi 20:00 = Los Angeles 08:00 (PST/PDT)
- Tbilisi 01:00 = Los Angeles 13:00 (PST/PDT)
- Best window: 20:00-03:00 Tbilisi time

The 8-12 hour time difference is Georgia's main downside for American nomads. Bank calls and government offices (IRS, Social Security) need to happen in your evening or late at night. Plan accordingly -- make those calls from your apartment, not a coworking space that closes at 20:00.

## Common Problems and Solutions

### Problem: Georgian SIM Stopped Working for International Calls

Some prepaid plans require activation of international calling as a separate feature.

**Solution:** Text or call your carrier's service number. For Magti, dial *111#. For Geocell, dial *100#. Confirm international calling is enabled on your plan.

### Problem: VoIP Call Quality Is Poor

Usually a WiFi issue, not a Georgia infrastructure issue.

**Solution:**
- Switch from cafe WiFi to mobile data (Georgian 4G is solid)
- Move closer to the router
- Close bandwidth-heavy apps (streaming, large downloads)
- Test at a different location -- if quality improves, the problem was the specific WiFi network

### Problem: US Bank Doesn't Recognize Georgian Phone Number

Banks sometimes flag calls or SMS from foreign numbers.

**Solution:** Use a browser-based service that provides a US caller ID. NomaPhone calls show a US number to the recipient. This matters for banks that screen incoming calls.

### Problem: Need to Receive SMS Verification Codes

Two-factor authentication is the bane of every expat's existence. Your US bank sends a code to your US number, but your US SIM is sitting in a drawer.

**Solution:** Keep a service that supports US SMS/2FA. NomaPhone and DialAnyone both handle this. Google Voice works too if you already have it set up. This is one area where YadaPhone falls short -- no SMS support.
