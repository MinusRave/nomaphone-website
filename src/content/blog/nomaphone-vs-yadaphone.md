## The Basics: What Both Services Share

Before diving into differences, here's what NomaPhone and YadaPhone have in common:

- **Browser-based calling.** No app download. Open your browser, buy credits, make a call.
- **Pay-as-you-go pricing.** No monthly subscription required.
- **Credits that never expire.** Buy $5 worth of credits today, use them in three years. Neither service pulls the "use it or lose it" trick.
- **No contracts.** No commitment, no cancellation process, no fine print.

If those four things are your only requirements, either service will work. The differences start mattering when you look closer at features, coverage, and pricing.

### Team Features and Virtual Numbers

YadaPhone offers a shared wallet and team analytics, plus US/Canada virtual numbers. If you're running a small remote team that needs centralized billing, or you want a number people can call you back on, YadaPhone is set up for that.

NomaPhone is outgoing calls only, for individuals: no team accounts and no phone numbers.

## Where NomaPhone Wins

Now for the differences that tip things in NomaPhone's direction.

### No Account, Any Browser

NomaPhone doesn't ask you to sign up. You buy $5, $10 or $20 of prepaid credit with a card, Apple Pay or Google Pay, and get a phone card code (also emailed to you) that holds the credit. It works in any modern browser, including Safari and Firefox, so a borrowed laptop or your phone's default browser is enough.

### US Toll-Free Numbers Included

NomaPhone can dial US and Canadian toll-free numbers (800, 888, 877, 866, 855, 844, 833) at its normal US rate. That matters when you need the IRS, a US bank's fraud line or an airline, and their main number is a 1-800 that won't connect from a foreign SIM.

### Refundable Credit, Per-Second Billing

Your NomaPhone credit never expires, and you can get unused credit refunded to your original payment method at any time, free. Calls are billed per second, with the per-minute rate shown on screen as you dial, so there are no surprises.

### Built for Nomads, Not Teams

This one's less about features and more about focus. NomaPhone is designed for one job: letting someone abroad call a phone number from any browser, with nothing to install or sign up for.

YadaPhone is a broader browser calling tool that also works for teams and businesses. That's not a knock on them. It just means the two products are aiming at different things.

## The Hidden Cost of No SMS/2FA

Price-per-minute comparisons look clean in a table. But they don't capture the full picture.

Neither service receives SMS or 2FA codes. NomaPhone makes outgoing calls only and doesn't give you a phone number, and YadaPhone, based on its published features, doesn't offer SMS/2FA receiving either. If your bank texts codes to your US number, you need a separate solution, like keeping a cheap US plan alive or switching to app-based authentication. Our guide to [keeping a US phone number abroad](/blog/maintain-us-phone-number-abroad/) covers the options.

Here's where calling still helps. You're in Bali. Your Chase account flags a suspicious login because you're accessing it from an Indonesian IP address. Chase sends a 6-digit verification code to your US phone number. You don't receive it because you're using a local SIM. You need to call Chase's fraud department, and the number on the back of your card is toll-free.

With NomaPhone you can dial that toll-free number from your browser at the normal US rate. One thing to know: NomaPhone calls are capped at 30 minutes, so if you're stuck on a very long hold, you'll need to dial again.

## Who Should Use YadaPhone

YadaPhone is the better choice if:

- **Price per minute is your top priority.** You make a high volume of calls and every cent counts.
- **You call countries NomaPhone doesn't cover.** YadaPhone's 150+ country list is wider than NomaPhone's [[countries]].
- **You need call recording.** You do client calls, interviews, or consultations that you want recorded and transcribed.
- **You need team features or a virtual number.** You manage a small team and want shared billing, or you want a US/Canada number people can call back.

## Who Should Use NomaPhone

NomaPhone is the better choice if:

- **You don't want another account.** Buy credit, get a code, call. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to install.
- **You need to reach US toll-free numbers.** Banks, the IRS, airlines and insurers often only publish 1-800 numbers.
- **You use Safari, Firefox or a borrowed device.** NomaPhone works in any modern browser.
- **You want simplicity.** No app, no team management, no feature bloat. Open your browser, buy credit, call. Unused credit can be refunded.

## Can You Use Both?

Yes. Neither service requires a subscription or contract. You could buy $5 in credit on each service and use them for different purposes.

Some people use YadaPhone for long, planned calls where recording is useful, and NomaPhone for quick calls to banks and toll-free lines from whatever browser is in front of them. There's no rule saying you have to pick one forever.

That said, most people prefer having one service they rely on. Fewer balances to track.

## The Bottom Line

YadaPhone is cheaper per minute, covers more countries, and has features NomaPhone doesn't — call recording, AI transcripts, team analytics, virtual numbers. If those matter to you, it's a strong service.

NomaPhone is the simpler tool: no account, any browser, US toll-free numbers included, per-second billing, and refundable credit that never expires.

The real question isn't "which service is cheaper?" It's "which problem am I actually trying to solve?"

If you're solving for call costs or coverage, YadaPhone wins.

If you're solving for making a call right now, from any browser, without signing up for anything, NomaPhone is built for exactly that.
