You need to call a factory in Shenzhen about a delayed shipment. Your supplier in Guangzhou isn't answering emails. Or your grandmother in Beijing wants to hear your voice. You open WhatsApp, tap call, and nothing happens.

Calling China from abroad works differently from calling almost anywhere else. The person on the other end lives behind the Great Firewall, so most of the apps you use with everyone else (WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, Google services) don't work for them without a VPN. Skype, the old fallback for calling phone numbers, shut down in May 2025.

The good news: a call to a Chinese **phone number** still goes through the ordinary phone network, and that isn't blocked. This guide covers how Chinese numbers are formatted, which methods actually connect, what they cost, and when to call.

## Why Calling China Is Different From Calling Anywhere Else

The Great Firewall is China's internet filtering system. It blocks or degrades many foreign internet services for people **inside** mainland China.

**Blocked or unreliable for people in mainland China (without a VPN):**
- WhatsApp (blocked since 2017)
- Telegram and Signal
- Google services, including Google Voice and Google Meet
- Facebook Messenger
- Many other foreign VoIP and video-calling apps

**What people in China actually use:**
- WeChat (Weixin), the default app for messages and app-to-app calls
- Their regular phone line from China Mobile, China Unicom, China Telecom or China Broadnet

The key distinction is **app-to-app versus phone network**. An app-to-app call (WhatsApp to WhatsApp) only works if both people can reach that app's servers, and from mainland China most of them can't. A call to a Chinese landline or mobile number travels over the international phone network and arrives like any other incoming international call. The Great Firewall doesn't stand in the way of that.

So you really have two practical choices: WeChat, if your contact uses it and you have an account, or a service that calls real Chinese phone numbers.

## Chinese Phone Numbers: Country Code, Area Codes, and Mobile Prefixes

Get the format wrong and the call won't connect, so this part is worth a minute.

### Country Code: +86

Every call to mainland China starts with **+86**. From a phone that doesn't support the "+" key, use your country's international exit code instead (011 from the US and Canada, 00 from the UK and most of Europe), then 86.

Inside China, landline area codes are written with a leading **0** (Beijing is 010, Shenzhen is 0755). That 0 is a domestic trunk prefix: **drop it** when dialing from abroad.

### Landline Area Codes

Chinese landline area codes are 2 or 3 digits once you remove the leading 0. Major cities:

| City | Domestic Format | Full International Format |
|---|---|---|
| Beijing | 010 | +86 10 XXXX XXXX |
| Shanghai | 021 | +86 21 XXXX XXXX |
| Guangzhou | 020 | +86 20 XXXX XXXX |
| Tianjin | 022 | +86 22 XXXX XXXX |
| Chongqing | 023 | +86 23 XXXX XXXX |
| Wuhan | 027 | +86 27 XXXX XXXX |
| Chengdu | 028 | +86 28 XXXX XXXX |
| Shenzhen | 0755 | +86 755 XXXX XXXX |
| Hangzhou | 0571 | +86 571 XXXX XXXX |
| Dongguan | 0769 | +86 769 XXXX XXXX |
| Suzhou | 0512 | +86 512 XXXX XXXX |

Two-digit area codes are followed by an 8-digit local number. Three-digit area codes are followed by a 7- or 8-digit local number (8 digits in most large cities).

### Mobile Numbers

Chinese mobile numbers are **11 digits and always start with 1** (for example 138, 159, 186, 189 or 199). There's no area code for mobiles.

To call a mobile from abroad: **+86 followed by all 11 digits.**

**Example:** a mobile number written as 138 1234 5678 is dialed as +86 138 1234 5678.

You'll find lists online matching prefixes to carriers (139 for China Mobile, 186 for China Unicom, 189 for China Telecom, and so on). Treat them as a rough guide only: mobile number portability has been available nationwide since 2019, so the prefix no longer reliably tells you the carrier. It doesn't matter for dialing anyway.

### Special Numbers: 400 and 800

Many Chinese companies publish **400** numbers (shared-cost customer service lines) and some still list older **800** toll-free numbers. These are usually set up for callers inside mainland China and typically can't be reached from abroad. Look for a regular landline number with an area code, often listed on the company's English site or under an "overseas" or "international" contact section.

### Common Dialing Mistakes

- Keeping the trunk zero: +86 **0**755... instead of +86 755...
- Adding a zero before a mobile number: +86 **0**138... (mobiles never take one)
- Adding an area code in front of a mobile number
- Wrong digit count: mobiles have exactly 11 digits; landlines are usually 10 or 11 digits after +86 including the area code
- Using +86 for Hong Kong (+852), Macau (+853) or Taiwan (+886)

## The Great Firewall and VoIP: Why Most Calling Apps Fail

Here is where most of the confusion comes from.

**If you're outside China calling a person in China with an app:**
The app has to work on their end too. WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal and Google-based apps are blocked for them, so the call fails, or works only while they're on a VPN. VPN calls tend to have delay, echo and drops, because the connection is being tunneled through a server somewhere else.

**If you're outside China calling a Chinese phone number:**
This is a normal international phone call. Whether you dial from your mobile carrier, a calling card or an internet calling service, the call reaches China over the phone network and rings their landline or mobile. Your end can run over the internet; their end is a regular phone. The Great Firewall doesn't block it.

**If you're inside China trying to call out:**
That's a different problem, covered further down. In short: foreign VoIP apps and websites may be blocked or unreliable from inside China, VPN or not.

One China-specific quirk is worth knowing: as part of a long-running anti-fraud campaign, Chinese carriers and phone security apps may flag incoming international calls, and many people simply don't pick up unknown foreign numbers. Send a WeChat message, text or email first so the person knows to expect your call.

## Carrier Roaming: Reliable but Expensive

Dialing +86 from your regular mobile plan just works, and call quality is usually good. The bill is the problem.

Without an international add-on, US and European carriers commonly charge somewhere between $1 and $3 or more per minute to Chinese numbers, and roaming rates while you're traveling can be higher still. Some carriers sell monthly international calling packages, and T-Mobile's Simple Global roaming has charged around $0.25/min for calls made while roaming in many countries. Pricing and plan names change often, so check your own carrier's current China rate before a long call.

For a quick call, carrier dialing is fine. For a 30-minute call with a supplier, pay-per-use carrier rates can easily run to tens of dollars.

## WeChat: Useful but Limited

WeChat is the default communication app in China, with well over a billion users. If your contact has WeChat, you can make free voice and video calls inside the app, and it's usually the best choice for regular calls with friends, family and established business contacts.

**WeChat works well for:**
- App-to-app voice and video calls
- Voice messages, text and file sharing
- Arranging a call time before you phone someone

**WeChat does NOT work for:**
- Calling Chinese landlines
- Calling mobile numbers of people who aren't on WeChat
- Reaching businesses, offices and services that only publish a phone number

Setting up WeChat from abroad can also be a hurdle. You can register with a non-Chinese phone number, but new accounts are sometimes asked to be verified by an existing WeChat user, and accounts can be restricted if they're flagged. If you don't already have WeChat, getting started can take some patience.

## Calling Cards: Old School but Functional

Physical and digital calling cards still work for calling China. Buy a card, dial a local access number, enter a PIN, then dial the Chinese number.

**Pros:**
- Low advertised per-minute rates to China
- Works from any phone, no internet needed

**Cons:**
- Access numbers and PINs every time
- Connection fees, maintenance fees and minute rounding
- Balances that expire
- Quality varies widely

A card advertising "1 cent a minute to China" may carry a per-call connection fee that wipes out the savings on short calls. Read the fine print.

## Calling China If You're Inside China (The VPN Question)

This guide is mainly about calling **into** China. If you're physically in mainland China and need to call out, the picture changes.

**The reality of VPNs in China:**
- Many VPNs are blocked or throttled, and reliability changes over time
- Even when a VPN connects, the extra latency often makes voice calls choppy
- Foreign calling apps and websites, browser-based ones included, may be blocked or unreliable from inside China

Don't count on any foreign internet calling service, NomaPhone included, working from inside mainland China. Arrange your calling setup before you travel, and have a fallback.

**What tends to work from inside China:**
- A Chinese SIM card from China Mobile, China Unicom or China Telecom, with international calling (check the carrier's current rates and add-ons)
- Your home carrier's roaming, if you can stomach the price
- WeChat for app-to-app calls

If you're in Hong Kong or Macau, these restrictions don't apply. They have their own phone systems and aren't behind the Great Firewall.

## Tips for Better Call Quality to China

### 1. Use a Stable Connection

With any internet calling service, your connection is the weak link. Avoid crowded hotel Wi-Fi at peak hours; a wired connection, a quiet coworking space or a strong mobile data signal is better. If you hear echo or dropouts, our guide to [fixing VoIP call quality](/blog/voip-quality-issues-fix/) walks through the usual fixes.

### 2. Know the Time Zone

All of China runs on one time zone: **China Standard Time, UTC+8**, with no daylight saving time. Harbin and Kashgar use the same clock as Beijing.

- **New York:** China is 13 hours ahead (12 hours during US daylight saving time)
- **Los Angeles:** 16 hours ahead (15 during US daylight saving time)
- **London:** 8 hours ahead (7 during British Summer Time)
- **Berlin/Paris:** 7 hours ahead (6 during European summer time)
- **Bangkok:** 1 hour ahead

From the US, the overlap with Chinese business hours falls in your evening: 9:00 AM in Beijing is 8:00 PM the previous day in New York in winter.

### 3. Call During Business Hours (and Avoid the Holidays)

- **Offices:** typically 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM, Monday to Friday, often with a lunch break of an hour or more around noon to 1:30 PM. Calls at lunchtime often go unanswered.
- **Factories:** often start around 8:00 AM, and many work Saturdays.
- **Government offices:** roughly 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM with a midday break, weekdays only.
- **Chinese New Year (Spring Festival):** the biggest shutdown of the year, falling in late January or February (in 2027 it's in early February). Offices close for about a week; many factories close for two weeks or more as workers travel home.
- **National Day Golden Week:** October 1 to 7. Most offices close.
- **Other public holidays:** Qingming (early April), Labor Day (around May 1), Dragon Boat Festival and Mid-Autumn Festival.

China also shifts some weekends around holidays to create longer breaks, so a Saturday or Sunday can be an official working day. Check the year's holiday calendar before planning an important call.

### 4. Have the Number Ready in the Right Format

- Landline: +86 [area code without the 0] [local number]
- Mobile: +86 [11-digit number starting with 1]

Save contacts with the +86 prefix so the number works from anywhere.

### 5. Prepare for Language Barriers

English is common at larger companies in Shanghai, Beijing and Shenzhen, and at export-focused manufacturers. Smaller factories and local offices may only speak Mandarin or a regional language such as Cantonese. Message first to arrange a time with the right English-speaking contact, and keep key details (order numbers, dates, quantities) ready to read out slowly or send in writing afterward.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use WhatsApp to call someone in China?

Usually not. WhatsApp is blocked in mainland China, so the person can only receive the call if they're on a VPN, and calls over a VPN tend to be unreliable. Call their phone number instead, or use WeChat.

### Do I need to dial a city code for Chinese mobile numbers?

No. Chinese mobile numbers are 11 digits starting with 1. Dial +86 followed by all 11 digits. Area codes are only for landlines.

### Is it cheaper to call a Chinese landline or mobile?

It depends on the provider. Some charge the same for both, others charge more for mobiles because mobile termination costs are often higher. Check landline and mobile rates separately. NomaPhone's China rate is [[rate:CN:both]].

### Can the person in China call me back?

Not through NomaPhone. NomaPhone only makes outgoing calls: it doesn't give you a phone number and can't receive calls. If you need a two-way channel, agree on WeChat or email for replies, or use your regular mobile number.

### Will the call work if I'm inside China?

This guide is about calling into China from abroad. From inside mainland China, foreign calling apps and websites may be blocked or unreliable, so don't rely on them. See the section on calling from inside China above.

### What about calling Hong Kong, Macau or Taiwan?

They have their own country codes: Hong Kong is +852, Macau +853 and Taiwan +886. Don't dial +86 for these numbers.
