## The IRS International Line: Your Most Important Number

The IRS has a dedicated international number for taxpayers abroad. This is the one to save.

**Number**: +1 267-941-1000

This is not a toll-free number — it's a direct-dial Philadelphia line that works from any country. Forget the 800 numbers you see on IRS notices. Those don't connect from outside the US.

### When to Call

IRS international line hours: Monday through Friday, 6:00 AM to 11:00 PM Eastern Time.

That's a wide window, which helps when you're dealing with a 12-hour time difference. If you're in Southeast Asia, aim for their early morning hours — that's evening your time and usually means shorter hold times.

### Expected Hold Times

During tax season (March through April 15), expect 30 to 60 minutes on hold. During extension season (September through October 15), hold times drop to 15 to 30 minutes. Off-season, you might get through in under 10 minutes.

### What You'll Need Ready

- Your Social Security Number
- Your most recent tax return (or at least the year and filing status)
- Your Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) if applicable
- Any IRS notice numbers (the letter code in the top right of IRS correspondence)
- A pen and paper — agents give you reference numbers fast

## State Tax Agency Numbers

If you have state tax obligations — and many nomads do, based on their last US domicile — you may need to call your state's tax agency too.

Here are the most common ones for digital nomads:

**California Franchise Tax Board**: +1 916-845-6500
Hours: Monday through Friday, 7:00 AM to 5:00 PM Pacific. Hold times: 20 to 45 minutes during tax season.

**New York State Tax Department**: +1 518-457-5181
Hours: Monday through Friday, 8:30 AM to 4:30 PM Eastern. Hold times: 15 to 40 minutes.

**Texas** — no state income tax. One less call to make.

**Florida** — also no state income tax. Lucky you.

**Illinois Department of Revenue**: +1 217-782-3336
Hours: Monday through Friday, 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM Central. Hold times: 10 to 30 minutes.

**Colorado Department of Revenue**: +1 303-238-7378
Hours: Monday through Friday, 8:00 AM to 4:30 PM Mountain. Hold times: 15 to 30 minutes.

None of these are toll-free from abroad. All of them are US landlines. All of them will keep you on hold.

### What You'll Need

- State taxpayer ID or SSN
- Previous year's state return
- Any correspondence or notice numbers
- Your current mailing address (even if it's overseas)

## Calling Your CPA or Tax Preparer

Here's the one that catches nomads off guard. Your CPA's office probably has a US landline. Maybe even a local number in Omaha or Phoenix or wherever they're based. They don't have a WhatsApp business account. They don't use Zoom for quick questions.

They want you to call their office number.

### The Timing Problem

Most CPA firms are slammed from February through April 15. If your preparer is working on your return and hits a question, they need an answer fast — not in 24 hours over email. A 10-minute call can save weeks of back-and-forth.

## Social Security Administration

If you receive Social Security benefits while abroad, or need to verify your earnings record for tax purposes, you'll need to reach the SSA.

**Number**: +1 800-772-1213

Wait — that's a toll-free number. It won't work from abroad, right? Correct. But there's a workaround.

**TTY line that works internationally**: +1 800-325-0778

For most nomads, the practical solution is to use a browser-based calling service that routes through US infrastructure. When your call originates from a US number, toll-free numbers work normally. NomaPhone and similar services handle this automatically.

### SSA Hours

Monday through Friday, 8:00 AM to 7:00 PM Eastern (local time).

Hold times: 30 to 60 minutes is standard. Budget your time accordingly.

### What You'll Need

- Your Social Security Number
- Date of birth
- Current mailing address on file with SSA
- Bank account information if you're updating direct deposit

## FBAR and FinCEN Reporting Questions

If you have foreign bank accounts totaling more than $10,000 at any point during the year, you're required to file an FBAR (Foreign Bank Account Report) with FinCEN. This catches a lot of nomads who open local bank accounts in the countries they live in.

**FinCEN Regulatory Help Line**: +1 800-767-2825

Again, toll-free — so you'll need a service that can connect through a US number.

**FinCEN FBAR Help Line**: +1 866-270-0733

Same deal. Toll-free, needs a US-originating call.

### Hours

Monday through Friday, 8:00 AM to 6:00 PM Eastern.

Hold times are generally shorter than IRS — typically 5 to 15 minutes.

### Common FBAR Questions Nomads Call About

- Whether a foreign retirement account counts toward the $10,000 threshold
- How to report accounts in countries with unusual banking structures
- Late filing procedures (the FBAR deadline is April 15, with an automatic extension to October 15)
- Whether cryptocurrency held on foreign exchanges needs reporting

## Estimated Tax Payment Issues

If you're self-employed abroad — and most digital nomads are — you probably make quarterly estimated tax payments. When a payment doesn't process, or you need to adjust your estimates, you'll need to call.

**IRS Estimated Tax Line**: +1 267-941-1000 (same international number)

When you get through, ask to be transferred to the estimated tax department.

## Tips for Smoother Tax Season Calls from Abroad

### 1. Call Early in the Morning (Their Time)

IRS hold times are shortest between 6:00 AM and 7:30 AM Eastern. If you're in Thailand, that's 5:00 PM to 6:30 PM — perfect timing. In Europe, it's late morning. Plan around it.

### 2. Have Everything Ready Before You Dial

You don't want to be on hold for 40 minutes, finally get a human, and then say "hold on, let me find my SSN." Have your documents open on screen before you pick up.

### 3. Use a Stable Internet Connection

If you're using VoIP, don't make your IRS call from a beach bar with spotty wifi. Use your coworking space or hotel room. A wired connection is even better if available.

### 4. Record Your Call (Where Legal)

Many VoIP services let you record calls. This is genuinely useful for tax calls where an agent gives you a case number, a deadline, or specific instructions. Check recording laws in your jurisdiction first.

### 5. Know the Phone Tree Shortcuts

For the IRS international line, here's a shortcut: after the language prompt, press 1, then wait through the automated messages. Don't press anything else until you hear the option to speak with a representative. The menu changes seasonally, but patience gets you to a human faster than button-mashing.

### 6. Time Zone Math

Do it the night before. Nothing worse than calling what you think is 9:00 AM Eastern and reaching a closed office because you forgot about daylight saving time. The US shifts clocks in March and November — and most of Asia and many countries in Europe don't.

## Frequently Asked Tax Season Questions

### Can I file my US taxes from abroad?

Yes. Every US citizen and green card holder must file regardless of where they live. You may qualify for the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (up to $126,500 for 2026) and the Foreign Tax Credit, which can reduce or eliminate your US tax bill.

### Do I need a US phone number to call the IRS?

No. The +1 267-941-1000 international line accepts calls from any number worldwide. However, for toll-free numbers like the SSA, you'll need a service that routes through US infrastructure.

### What if my CPA won't take international calls?

Some office phone systems block international numbers. A browser calling service with a US virtual number solves this — your call shows up as a domestic US number on their caller ID.

### Is the October 15 deadline automatic for nomads?

No. The automatic extension for Americans abroad is only to June 15. To get to October 15, you need to file Form 4868 by April 15 (or June 15 if you qualify for the automatic abroad extension).

### Do I need to report my foreign bank accounts?

If your combined foreign accounts exceeded $10,000 at any point during the year, yes. File an FBAR with FinCEN by April 15 (automatic extension to October 15). Penalties for not filing are steep — up to $10,000 per violation for non-willful failures.
