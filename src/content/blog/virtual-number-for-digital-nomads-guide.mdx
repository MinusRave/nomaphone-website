You cross a border, swap your SIM card, and within 24 hours you're locked out of your bank account, your crypto exchange, and your work Slack—all because the 2FA code went to a number you no longer have. This is not a rare edge case. It happens to digital nomads constantly, and it's entirely preventable.

A permanent virtual number for digital nomads is the single most practical fix for this problem.

---

## The Real Cost of Relying on Local SIMs

Picking up a local SIM in each new country feels like the smart, budget-conscious move. For data, it often is. For maintaining a stable identity tied to a phone number, it's a liability.

Here's what you stand to lose every time you retire a local SIM:

- **Bank and financial account access** — Most banks require SMS verification to log in or approve transactions. Your number changes; your access breaks.
- **Government and tax portals** — If you're registered with a tax authority or social security system back home, those portals don't care that you're in Chiang Mai now.
- **Two-factor authentication (2FA)** — Google, Microsoft, PayPal, and hundreds of other services tie 2FA to a specific phone number. Changing that number mid-trip creates a recovery nightmare.
- **Client and employer contact** — Handing out a new number every six weeks is unprofessional and causes missed calls.

International roaming is the other common fallback, but the math rarely works out. Roaming charges from major carriers can run $2–$10 per minute for calls, and that's before you factor in the monthly cost of keeping your home country plan active just so the number doesn't get recycled.

## What a Virtual Number Actually Gives You

A virtual number is a real phone number—assigned to a specific country or region—that isn't tied to a physical SIM card. It lives in the cloud. You keep the same number whether you're in Berlin, Bali, or Buenos Aires.

The practical benefits are straightforward:

- **One permanent number for your entire nomadic life.** Banks, clients, and government offices always reach you at the same digits.
- **SMS 2FA keeps working.** Your verification codes arrive at a number you actually control and can always access—as long as your bank accepts it (see the caveat below).
- **Incoming calls follow you.** Most providers ring an app or forward calls over the internet.
- **Port or keep your existing home country number** with some providers, so contacts don't even notice you've moved.

**Where to get one:** Google Voice (for US residents, set up before you leave), number-hosting apps like Hushed, low-cost US carriers like Tello or US Mobile that let you keep your existing number cheaply and receive texts over Wi-Fi calling, eSIM providers that include a number, and number-parking services if you're keeping a UK number. Prices typically run from free to a few dollars a month.

**The caveat:** many banks and some government portals reject VoIP numbers for 2FA. Test each important account before you rely on a virtual number. Often the most robust setup is keeping your original carrier number alive on the cheapest plan (or as an eSIM) and using a virtual number for everything else.

## The SMS 2FA Problem Deserves Its Own Attention

This point is worth repeating because it catches people off guard at the worst possible moment.

Modern account security is heavily dependent on SMS-based two-factor authentication. It's not perfect security, but it's what most financial institutions, government portals, and large platforms use. When your phone number changes, your access to these systems is at risk—not just inconvenienced, but sometimes completely blocked until you go through a lengthy manual recovery process.

A permanent number—your original one kept alive, or a virtual number your accounts accept—means your 2FA baseline never moves. Every SMS verification code lands in one place, regardless of where on earth you currently are. Where you can, also switch accounts to authenticator apps or push approvals, which don't depend on a phone number at all.
