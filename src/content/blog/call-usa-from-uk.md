## What UK Carriers Charge for US Calls

Let's start with the expensive option — the one your phone is already set up for.

### EE

EE charges roughly £1 to £2 per minute for international calls to the US on standard pay monthly and PAYG plans. Some newer plans bundle a handful of international minutes, but most customers pay the full per-minute rate.

A 30-minute call on EE's standard rate: £30 to £60.

That's before the hold music even ends.

### Vodafone

Vodafone sits in a similar range. Standard international rates run £1 to £3 per minute for US calls, depending on your specific plan. They offer an International Calling add-on for around £5/month that lowers the per-minute rate, but it comes with limited minutes.

Without the add-on, a 30-minute call: up to £90.

### Three

Three has built a reputation for being more international-friendly than other UK networks. Their Go Roam scheme is genuinely useful when travelling. But for calling US numbers from the UK on a standard plan, you're still looking at £1 to £2 per minute.

Three does occasionally offer international calling bolt-ons. Worth checking if you're already a customer.

### BT and Virgin Media Landlines

If you have a BT home phone, their Anytime International add-on costs around £8.50 per month and includes minutes to US landlines and mobiles. Virgin Media's International Extra is similar at roughly £7 per month.

Decent if you call the US regularly and don't mind being tethered to your house phone. Less practical for anyone who doesn't own a landline — which is most people under 45.

### The Pattern Here

UK carrier rates to the US are designed for people who make one accidental international call per year and don't notice the charge. If you're calling the US with any regularity — for work, for banking, for family — carrier rates will cost you hundreds of pounds annually.

## Every Method for Calling the US From the UK (2026)

Here's a full breakdown of what actually works, what it costs, and the trade-offs you should know about.

### WhatsApp and FaceTime (Free, But Limited)

If the person you're calling has WhatsApp or FaceTime, these work perfectly over WiFi or mobile data. No per-minute charges. No setup beyond the apps you probably already have.

The limitation is obvious. You can only call people who use the app. Chase Bank doesn't have WhatsApp. The IRS doesn't accept FaceTime calls. Your US insurance company's claims line is a regular phone number.

For personal calls to tech-savvy friends and family: WhatsApp is great. For anything that involves a real US phone number — banks, businesses, government agencies, customer service lines — it's not an option.

### Google Voice

Google Voice lets US residents make free calls to US and Canadian numbers. If you're an American expat who set up Google Voice before moving to the UK, it might still work.

The catch: Google Voice requires a US phone number to sign up. If you're British and never had a US number, you can't create an account. Google has been tightening restrictions for years.

Even with an existing account, call quality from the UK is inconsistent. Lag, echo, and dropped connections happen. Google built this for someone in Michigan, not Manchester. Keep using it if it works for you, but don't rely on it as your only option.

### Skype Is Gone

Microsoft shut down Skype in May 2025 and moved users to Microsoft Teams. If you found an old article recommending Skype, that info is out of date.

### Microsoft Teams Phone

Teams inherited some of Skype's calling features. You can call real US numbers, but it requires a Microsoft 365 subscription plus a calling plan add-on. For businesses already on Microsoft 365, this can work. For personal use, it's like buying a lorry to pick up groceries.

### WiFi Calling Through Your UK Carrier

Most UK carriers now support WiFi calling, which lets you make calls over your home WiFi instead of the mobile network. This can improve call quality in areas with weak signal.

Here's what WiFi calling does not do: it does not change the per-minute rate. A call to a US number over WiFi costs the same as a call over the mobile network. WiFi calling is about coverage, not price.

Don't confuse WiFi calling with VoIP. They sound similar but work very differently for your wallet.

### Viber Out

Viber Out lets you call real phone numbers from the Viber app. Rates to the US run a few pence per minute — much cheaper than carrier rates. But you need the app installed, quality can be hit-or-miss, and Viber constantly pushes its social features when you just want to make a call. It works, but it's clunky.

### International Calling Cards

These still exist. You buy a card (physical or digital), dial an access number, then enter the destination number. Advertised rates look cheap — sometimes just pence per minute.

The reality: hidden connection fees, maintenance charges, and expiration dates buried in the terms. Some round every call up to five-minute blocks. That advertised 1p/min turns into something much higher once fees stack up. Most people would rather not.

## Tips for Better Call Quality From the UK

Whichever service you choose, these tips will help.

### Use WiFi Over Mobile Data

UK broadband is generally solid. WiFi gives you more consistent quality than mobile data — especially important for long hold-time calls.

### Plug In Headphones

Your laptop's built-in mic picks up keyboard noise and the telly in the next room. Basic earbuds with a microphone make a big difference.

### Grant Microphone Permissions

The first time you use a browser calling service, your browser asks for microphone access. Click "Allow." If you accidentally blocked it, check site settings via the lock icon in the address bar.

### Mind the Time Difference

The UK is five hours ahead of US Eastern Time and eight hours ahead of Pacific. East Coast offices close at 5pm ET (10pm London). West Coast closes at 5pm PT (1am your time). The IRS phone lines open at 7am ET (noon London) and close at 7pm ET (midnight).

### Have the Number Ready

Format: +1 followed by the 10-digit number. For example, +1 555 123 4567. Most VoIP services handle formatting automatically.
