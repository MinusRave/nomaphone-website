You moved abroad. Maybe it was Lisbon, Chiang Mai, or Buenos Aires. But your US bank still texts that old number. Your brokerage sends 2FA codes to it. Half your accounts use it to recover your password. Losing that number isn't just inconvenient: it can lock you out of critical accounts.

Here's how to keep your US phone number working while living outside the country, and what breaks with each option.

## Why Your US Number Matters More Than You Think

A US number is often tied to:

- **Bank and brokerage 2FA**: one-time codes sent by SMS to verify sign-ins, transfers and new devices
- **Account recovery**: email, Apple, Google and payment accounts often use it to reset your password
- **Government and tax logins**: services such as ID.me and Login.gov, used for IRS and Social Security online accounts, can use your phone for verification
- **Contacts back home**: family, clients and doctors who already have the number

If the number goes dead, recovering accounts that rely on it can take days or weeks of support calls.

## The Problem with Traditional Roaming

Keeping your existing plan active abroad sounds simple. It's expensive.

The big US carriers typically charge a daily international pass on days you use the line abroad, and full international plans cost far more than a line you mostly use to receive texts. Prices change, so check your carrier's current rates.

There's also a long-term catch: carriers' terms generally let them limit or end roaming if most of your usage is abroad over an extended period. A plan built for vacations isn't built for living overseas.

Paying a full monthly plan just to hold a number is a tax on being an expat.

## Your Options for Keeping a US Number Abroad

### Option 1: Move Your Number to a Cheap Prepaid Plan

For most expats this is the safest choice. You port your number to a low-cost prepaid plan from an MVNO (Tello, US Mobile, Mint Mobile, Ultra Mobile, Red Pocket and others) and keep it on the cheapest tier that includes texting. Check current pricing; it changes often.

Why it works: the number **stays a real mobile number**, so banks keep sending codes to it. Put the line on an eSIM in your phone, turn on **Wi-Fi calling**, and texts and calls reach you over Wi-Fi without roaming, on carriers and phones that support it.

Watch out for: auto-renew (a lapsed prepaid line can release your number), each plan's roaming terms, and whether Wi-Fi calling works with your phone model.

### Option 2: Port Your Number to Google Voice

Google Voice can take over your existing mobile number for a one-time porting fee (check current pricing). Calls and texts then arrive in the Google Voice app over the internet, anywhere.

The caveats matter:

- **You need a Google Voice account first**, and signing up requires a US number and generally being in the US. Do it before you leave.
- **Porting ends your carrier service**, and the number becomes a VoIP number.
- **Many banks reject VoIP numbers** for 2FA, and short-code texts often don't reach Google Voice. Accounts that worked with your old number may stop sending codes.
- **Inactivity**: Google can reclaim numbers that go unused for an extended period.

Good for keeping the number reachable for people; risky as your only number for banking. More in [why Google Voice stops working abroad](/blog/google-voice-not-working-abroad/).

### Option 3: Park Your Number with a Parking Service

Number parking means porting your number to a service whose job is just to hold it, such as NumberBarn, for a small monthly fee. Depending on the plan, calls can go to voicemail or be forwarded, and some plans forward texts to email.

Parking is the cheapest way to make sure the number isn't lost. But like Google Voice, a parked number usually shows up as non-mobile or VoIP in carrier lookups, so bank 2FA may stop working. Park once your critical accounts no longer depend on SMS to that number.

### Option 4: Keep Your Current Carrier, Scaled Down

Ask your carrier about a cheaper plan, a prepaid plan from the same carrier, or a temporary suspension. Some carriers let you suspend a line for a limited period while keeping the number. Ask exactly what happens to the number and to texts during a suspension.

### Option 5: Start Fresh with a New Virtual Number

If your old number is already gone, an app-based US virtual number takes minutes to set up. It's a VoIP number with the same bank limitations, and you'll need to update it everywhere.

### How to Port Without Losing the Number

1. **Don't cancel your current plan first.** Canceling before porting can release the number permanently.
2. **Get your account number and port-out PIN** from your current carrier.
3. **Start the port from the new provider.** It often completes within hours to a few business days.
4. **Confirm calls and texts work** on the new provider before you touch the old account.
5. **Port while you're still in the US** if you can, so activation texts and support calls are easy.

### Which Option Should You Pick?

| Option | Banks treat it as mobile? | Ongoing cost | Watch out for |
|--------|---------------------------|--------------|---------------|
| Cheap prepaid plan | Usually yes | Low monthly fee | Missed refills |
| Google Voice | Often no | Free after porting fee | VoIP rejection, inactivity |
| Number parking | Often no | Small monthly fee | VoIP rejection |
| Current carrier, scaled down | Yes | Higher | Roaming limits |
| New virtual number | Often no | Varies | New number everywhere |

## Handling SMS 2FA When You're Abroad

You log into your bank from Lisbon and it sends a code to a US number that's in a drawer, switched off, or gone.

What works:

- **Real mobile number plus Wi-Fi calling**: keep the US line on a prepaid plan, in your phone's eSIM slot, with data roaming off and Wi-Fi calling on. Use a local SIM or travel eSIM for data.
- **Authenticator apps, passkeys and in-app approval**: switch every account that allows it before you leave, and save the backup codes.
- **Foreign number as a backup**: some services accept a non-US number. Many US banks don't.

What breaks: once your number moves to Google Voice, a parking service or another VoIP provider, many banks and payment apps stop sending codes to it. Move those accounts to an app-based method first, then switch the number.

## What to Do Before You Leave

If you're still in the US and planning a move abroad, handle this before you get on the plane:

- Audit which accounts use your US number for 2FA and recovery
- Switch accounts to authenticator apps, passkeys or in-app approval where possible
- Choose your option (prepaid, Google Voice or parking) and port before canceling anything
- Turn on Wi-Fi calling and test receiving a code with airplane mode on and Wi-Fi connected
- Lock your carrier account against port-outs and SIM swaps
- Save your bank's and carrier's phone numbers, and set up a way to call US toll-free lines from abroad

If you're already abroad and your number is gone, contact your former carrier immediately. A recently canceled number can sometimes be recovered.
